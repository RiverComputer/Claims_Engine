# SDG 15 — Life on Land
## Validation Schema Reference

**Schema type:** `sdg_15_life_on_land`
**Targets:** SDG 15.1, 15.3, 15.5, 15.8, 15.9
**Source:** UN Global Indicator Framework — unstats.un.org/sdgs/indicators

**Purpose:** This schema evaluates whether evidence demonstrates protection, restoration, and sustainable governance of terrestrial and inland freshwater ecosystems. It is designed for validation of land stewardship claims — community conservancies, restoration zones, protected corridors, or any area under documented ecological governance.

---

## Overview

SDG 15 asks communities and nations to protect, restore, and promote the sustainable use of terrestrial ecosystems — forests, wetlands, drylands, and mountains — and to halt biodiversity loss. At the community level, this becomes a question of whether a defined area is under genuine stewardship: known, governed, monitored, and actively protected.

This schema focuses on **area-based stewardship** — the governance of a specific piece of land or ecosystem. It is not sufficient to demonstrate ecological interest; evidence must show that governance exists and is functional, that ecological condition is being tracked, and that harmful pressures are being managed.

---

## Criteria

### SDG15_1 — Area-based stewardship established *(required)*

**What this asks:** Is the stewardship area under defined governance with conservation intent?

This is the foundational criterion. Evidence must demonstrate three things together: a documented boundary, a governance structure with identified actors, and stated management objectives oriented toward ecological outcomes.

**What satisfies it:**
- A community conservancy, indigenous and community conserved area (ICCA), or equivalent with documented boundary (map, GPS coordinates, or agreed landmarks)
- A governance structure — committee, council, or custodian group — with named members and a stated mandate
- A management plan or set of objectives describing intended ecological outcomes
- Evidence that the governance structure has been operational (meeting records, activity logs)
- Legal or customary recognition of the area's protected status

**What does not satisfy it:**
- Informal stewardship without any documentation of boundary or governance
- A governance structure that was constituted but has not met or acted
- Areas described as protected without evidence of actual protective activity
- Boundary claims without any management intent or ecological objective

---

### SDG15_2 — No net land degradation demonstrated *(required)*

**What this asks:** Is the stewardship area free from net land degradation, with restoration documented where needed?

Evidence must demonstrate that land within the stewardship area has not undergone net degradation — meaning the ecological condition has been maintained or improved — and that where past degradation exists, restoration activities are documented.

**What satisfies it:**
- Vegetation cover assessments showing stability or improvement over time
- Comparison of current condition to a documented baseline (photos, maps, surveys)
- Documented restoration activities — planting, erosion control, drainage restoration — with evidence of follow-through
- Remote sensing analysis or participatory mapping showing land cover change
- Evidence of reduced encroachment, agricultural conversion, or degradation pressure

**What does not satisfy it:**
- Assertions that the area is "well protected" without evidential basis
- Restoration plans without evidence of implementation
- Condition assessments without temporal comparison
- Evidence of ongoing degradation without documented response

---

### SDG15_3 — Biodiversity monitored *(required)*

**What this asks:** Is biodiversity being systematically observed, with records enabling trend detection?

Evidence must demonstrate that some form of biodiversity observation is occurring within the stewardship area, with documented baseline and repeat records that would allow an evaluator to detect change over time.

**What satisfies it:**
- Species lists (plants, mammals, birds, reptiles, insects, amphibians) with observation dates and locations
- Camera trap data with documented placement and retrieval records
- Participatory biodiversity surveys with community monitors
- Key species abundance records (nesting pairs, track counts, call surveys)
- Partnership with a research institution producing systematic biodiversity records
- Transect surveys or quadrat sampling with repeat methodology

**What does not satisfy it:**
- General descriptions of biodiversity ("many birds were seen") without species-level or count-level data
- A single biodiversity survey with no subsequent monitoring
- Species lists without observation dates, making temporal analysis impossible
- Biodiversity assessments of adjacent areas not within the stewardship boundary

---

### SDG15_4 — Ecosystem services documented and integrated into planning *(recommended)*

**What this asks:** Have the ecosystem services provided by the area been assessed and used in decision-making?

Evidence demonstrates that the benefits provided by the stewardship area — water regulation, carbon storage, food provision, pollination, spiritual value — have been assessed and that this assessment has informed at least one documented governance or planning decision.

**What satisfies it:**
- A formal or participatory ecosystem services assessment
- Community knowledge documentation identifying services provided by the area
- Evidence that an ecosystem services assessment influenced a planning or governance decision (land use decisions, management plan objectives, budget allocations)
- Valuation or mapping of services provided to adjacent communities
- Carbon or biodiversity offset documentation linked to demonstrated ecosystem function

**What does not satisfy it:**
- General statements about the importance of the ecosystem without documentation
- Assessments conducted but not connected to any governance decision
- Carbon credit claims without underlying ecological evidence

---

### SDG15_5 — Invasive species assessed and managed *(recommended)*

**What this asks:** Have invasive alien species been assessed and are management measures in place?

Evidence demonstrates that the presence or absence of invasive alien species within the stewardship area has been assessed, and that where invasives are present, management or prevention measures are in place with documented follow-through.

**What satisfies it:**
- An invasive species survey with species identified, mapped, and dated
- Documented removal activities (manual clearance, controlled burning, mechanical removal) with follow-up monitoring
- Prevention measures (buffer zone management, visitor protocols, seed source controls)
- Community monitoring for early detection of new invasions
- Partnership with an agency or institution providing technical support for invasive management
- Evidence that management activities have reduced invasive cover or spread

**What does not satisfy it:**
- General awareness that invasives are present without systematic assessment
- Removal activities without evidence of planning or follow-up monitoring
- Assessments limited to adjacent land rather than the stewardship area itself

## Verdicts

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Satisfied** | All required criteria met; recommended criteria addressed where applicable |
| **Partial** | Required criteria mostly met with minor gaps; recommended criteria partially addressed |
| **Not satisfied** | One or more required criteria not met |
| **Pending** | Evaluation not yet completed |

Required criteria (SDG15_1, SDG15_2, SDG15_3) must be satisfied for an overall verdict of **Satisfied**. SDG15_4 and SDG15_5 are strongly recommended and their presence strengthens the case for verification, but their absence alone does not block a Satisfied verdict.

---

## Relationship to Other Schemas

SDG 15 is the primary schema for land stewardship claims. It frequently co-applies with SDG 6 where stewardship areas include inland water bodies or catchments.

The Obuntu **Re-Naturing** pathway addresses the same ecological restoration from a relational perspective — the restoration of community relationship with land and ecosystem — rather than conformance with an external standard. The Obuntu **Re-Landing** pathway addresses the governance and tenure dimensions that underpin SDG15_1.

Where stewardship governance is the primary claim, SDG 16 criteria may also apply.
