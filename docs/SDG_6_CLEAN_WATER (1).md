# SDG 6 — Clean Water and Sanitation
## Validation Schema Reference

**Schema type:** `sdg_6_clean_water`
**Targets:** SDG 6.3, 6.5, 6.6, 6.b
**Source:** UN Global Indicator Framework — unstats.un.org/sdgs/indicators

**Purpose:** This schema evaluates whether evidence demonstrates contribution to sustainable water management and protection of water-related ecosystems. It is designed for use in community-level validation of interventions affecting water bodies — rivers, lakes, wetlands, or catchments.

---

## Overview

SDG 6 commits nations and communities to ensuring the availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all. At the community level, this translates into a set of observable commitments: knowing the condition of your water, acting against what harms it, and governing it together.

This schema is not a checklist of project activities. It is a conformance evaluation — it asks whether the **evidence** attached to a validation node demonstrates each criterion to a sufficient standard for an external evaluator to make a judgement.

---

## Criteria

### SDG6_1 — Water quality monitored *(required)*

**What this asks:** Has the quality of the water body been measured and documented?

Evidence must demonstrate that the water body's condition has been observed or tested in a way that establishes a baseline for tracking change over time. This is the foundational criterion — without it, no other claims about improvement or protection can be verified.

**What satisfies it:**
- Water quality test results (turbidity, pH, dissolved oxygen, bacterial counts, heavy metals)
- Documented observation records from trained community monitors
- Photographic or video evidence with date and location metadata
- Partnership with a laboratory or environmental agency producing documented results
- Community mapping records noting observable indicators (colour, odour, flow, aquatic life)

**What does not satisfy it:**
- General statements that water is "clean" or "safe" without supporting measurement
- Plans to conduct monitoring that has not yet taken place
- Single undated observations without recurrence

---

### SDG6_2 — Pollution or discharge addressed *(required)*

**What this asks:** Have sources of pollution been identified and acted upon?

Evidence must identify specific pollution sources or harmful discharge affecting the water body, and document that action — or a binding agreement to act — has been taken to reduce or prevent that harm.

**What satisfies it:**
- Named pollution sources (agricultural runoff, pit latrines, industrial discharge, solid waste dumping)
- Documented cleanup activities with before/after comparison
- Formal commitments (signed agreements, local government resolutions, community bylaws)
- Community enforcement actions against observed polluters
- Photographs documenting removal of pollution sources

**What does not satisfy it:**
- General concern about pollution without identification of specific sources
- Agreements about future action without evidence those agreements were honoured
- Awareness campaigns alone, without accompanying protective action

---

### SDG6_3 — Water-related ecosystem condition documented *(required)*

**What this asks:** Is the extent and condition of the ecosystem recorded in a usable form?

Evidence must establish the condition and extent of the water-related ecosystem — wetland, river, lake, or catchment — in a form that enables future comparison and change detection. This is the spatial and ecological counterpart to SDG6_1.

**What satisfies it:**
- Maps of the ecosystem boundary (paper, digital, GPS-traced)
- Vegetation surveys or wetland condition assessments
- Species lists (plants, birds, fish, amphibians) with observation dates
- Hydrological records (seasonal water levels, flow measurements)
- Community-produced transect walks or participatory mapping outputs

**What does not satisfy it:**
- Maps produced without community input that are not tied to any management action
- General descriptions of the landscape without measurable or spatial specificity
- Condition reports for adjacent land without focus on the water body itself

---

### SDG6_4 — Water management governance in place *(recommended)*

**What this asks:** Does some form of structured water governance exist?

Evidence demonstrates that a plan, coordination body, or management mechanism for the water body has been established, with documented activity showing it is functional and not merely nominal.

**What satisfies it:**
- A water management plan with named responsible parties and a timeline
- A formally constituted water management committee with meeting records
- A local government bylaw or conservation order with enforcement activity
- A multi-stakeholder coordination mechanism (upstream-downstream committees, catchment forums)
- Evidence that decisions made in a governance forum have been implemented

**What does not satisfy it:**
- A management plan that has never been acted upon
- A committee that has only met once with no follow-up
- Governance structures that exist on paper but have no documented activity

---

### SDG6_5 — Community participation in water governance *(recommended)*

**What this asks:** Have community members meaningfully participated in water management decisions?

Evidence demonstrates not just that consultation happened, but that community participation has been designed into governance structures in a formal, operational way — and that this participation has influenced at least one documented outcome.

**What satisfies it:**
- Meeting records showing community members present and contributing to governance decisions
- Evidence that community input changed a specific decision (documented before/after positions)
- Inclusion of women, youth, and marginalised groups in decision-making roles
- Community monitoring data used in governance decision-making
- Formal representation of community members on water management bodies

**What does not satisfy it:**
- One-way information events (briefings, announcements) without two-way dialogue
- Attendance at meetings without documented contribution or influence
- Tokenistic inclusion without evidence of substantive participation

---

## Verdicts

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Satisfied** | All required criteria met; recommended criteria addressed where applicable |
| **Partial** | Required criteria mostly met with minor gaps; recommended criteria partially addressed |
| **Not satisfied** | One or more required criteria not met |
| **Pending** | Evaluation not yet completed |

Required criteria (SDG6_1, SDG6_2, SDG6_3) must be satisfied for an overall verdict of **Satisfied**. SDG6_4 and SDG6_5 are strongly recommended but their absence alone does not block a Satisfied verdict.

---

## Relationship to Other Schemas

SDG 6 and SDG 15 frequently co-apply — water bodies are embedded in terrestrial ecosystems. A validation node addressing a wetland restoration project may satisfy criteria under both schemas simultaneously. Where a validation node references freshwater ecosystem governance, SDG 16 criteria may also be relevant.

The Obuntu **Re-Naturing** pathway is the relational counterpart to SDG 6 within this framework — it evaluates the same ecological restoration from the perspective of community relationship with land and water, rather than conformance with an external standard.
