# SDG 16 — Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions
## Validation Schema Reference

**Schema type:** `sdg_16_strong_institutions`
**Targets:** SDG 16.3, 16.6, 16.7, 16.10
**Source:** UN Global Indicator Framework — unstats.un.org/sdgs/indicators

**Purpose:** This schema evaluates whether evidence demonstrates access to justice, effective and accountable governance, inclusive participatory decision-making, and public access to information. It is designed for validation of governance and institutional claims — community organisations, conservation committees, local governance bodies, or any structure claiming to exercise collective authority on behalf of a community.

---

## Overview

SDG 16 asks for peaceful, just, and inclusive societies with effective, accountable, and transparent institutions. At the community level, this translates into a specific set of observable commitments: that people can access fair processes when disputes arise, that governance bodies represent those they serve, that decisions are made with genuine participation, that money is tracked honestly, and that information is not hoarded.

This schema is not an evaluation of whether governance is good or whether outcomes are just. It is a conformance evaluation — it asks whether the **evidence** attached to a validation node demonstrates that these structural features of accountable governance exist and are operational.

---

## Criteria

### SDG16_1 — Dispute resolution mechanism accessible *(required)*

**What this asks:** Does an accessible dispute resolution mechanism exist and is it operational?

Evidence must demonstrate that a formal or informal mechanism for resolving disputes — between community members, between the community and external actors, or between members and the governance body itself — is accessible, has been used, and communicates outcomes to the parties involved.

**What satisfies it:**
- Records of a dispute being brought to and resolved through a named mechanism (committee hearing, elder council, community court, mediation process)
- Evidence that the mechanism is known and accessible to community members (notices, documented referrals)
- Documentation that outcomes were communicated to the parties involved
- Bylaws, rules, or terms of reference describing the dispute process
- Evidence that a mechanism previously lacking has been formally established as part of this work

**What does not satisfy it:**
- General statements that disputes are handled informally without documentation of any process
- A named dispute mechanism that has never been invoked or tested
- Outcomes communicated to governance body but not to the disputing parties
- Mechanisms accessible only to some members of the community

---

### SDG16_2 — Governance body is representative and inclusive *(required)*

**What this asks:** Does the composition of the governance body reflect the community it serves?

Evidence demonstrates that the governance body's membership reflects demographic diversity — including women, youth, and historically marginalised groups — or that documented steps have been taken to address gaps in representation.

**What satisfies it:**
- A membership list or attendance record showing gender balance, age diversity, or inclusion of marginalised groups
- A formal inclusion policy or representation requirement embedded in governance rules
- Documented recruitment or election processes designed to achieve representative composition
- Evidence that previously underrepresented groups now hold named roles
- Recorded acknowledgement of representation gaps and a documented plan to address them

**What does not satisfy it:**
- Claims of representativeness without a membership list or evidence of process
- A single woman or young person on a body otherwise dominated by a single demographic
- Inclusion of marginalised groups as observers without voice or vote
- Representative composition on paper that is contradicted by attendance records

---

### SDG16_3 — Participatory decision-making documented *(required)*

**What this asks:** Have community members had structured opportunities to participate in governance decisions, and has that participation influenced outcomes?

Evidence demonstrates not just that meetings happened, but that structured participation — meaningful, two-way, with real influence — shaped at least one documented governance decision.

**What satisfies it:**
- Meeting minutes showing community input on a specific question and a decision influenced by that input
- Evidence of a decision changed or revised following community feedback (documented before/after positions)
- A participatory planning process with documented outputs that were incorporated into a governance document
- Community voting records on a specific question
- Documented acknowledgement by governance leadership that a community position changed a decision
- Facilitated consultations with documented outputs that were formally presented to the governance body

**What does not satisfy it:**
- One-way information sharing (briefings, announcements) without recorded community response
- Community attendance at meetings without documented contribution or influence on any decision
- Post-hoc consultation where decisions had already been made
- Large meetings without structured facilitation that produced no documented output

---

### SDG16_4 — Financial accountability demonstrated *(recommended)*

**What this asks:** Does the governance body plan and track expenditure, with records accessible to the community?

Evidence demonstrates that financial planning and tracking is in place and that community members can access financial records — not necessarily that finances are perfect, but that there is a system and that the system is open.

**What satisfies it:**
- A budget document covering the governance period, with documented approval by the governance body
- Income and expenditure records or accounts — even simple hand-written ledgers with receipts
- A public financial report presented to community members with documented distribution or presentation
- An audit or financial review conducted by an independent party
- Evidence that community members requested and received financial information
- Bank statements or mobile money records aligned to documented activity

**What does not satisfy it:**
- Financial records maintained only by leadership without any mechanism for community access
- Budget documents produced but never adopted or published
- Claims of financial transparency without any supporting documentation
- Records only of income without corresponding expenditure documentation

---

### SDG16_5 — Governance information publicly accessible *(recommended)*

**What this asks:** Are governance decisions, plans, and financial summaries made accessible to community members?

Evidence demonstrates that the governance body proactively makes information available — decisions, plans, financial summaries — in formats that community members can access and understand, with a mechanism for requesting further information.

**What satisfies it:**
- Noticeboards, community radio broadcasts, WhatsApp groups, or printed bulletins distributing governance information
- Meeting minutes distributed or posted after each meeting
- An annual report or governance summary accessible to the broader community
- A named person or process through which community members can request further information
- Evidence that community members have accessed and used governance information
- Documents produced in local languages or accessible oral formats where literacy is a barrier

**What does not satisfy it:**
- Information available only upon request without any proactive publication
- Governance documents accessible only in a single physical location that is not reliably accessible
- Information shared only with governance body members and not the broader community
- Documents produced in formats inaccessible to the majority of community members

## Verdicts

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Satisfied** | All required criteria met; recommended criteria addressed where applicable |
| **Partial** | Required criteria mostly met with minor gaps; recommended criteria partially addressed |
| **Not satisfied** | One or more required criteria not met |
| **Pending** | Evaluation not yet completed |

Required criteria (SDG16_1, SDG16_2, SDG16_3) must be satisfied for an overall verdict of **Satisfied**. SDG16_4 and SDG16_5 are strongly recommended — their presence significantly strengthens the credibility of governance claims — but their absence alone does not block a Satisfied verdict.

## Relationship to Other Schemas

SDG 16 is the governance schema. It applies wherever a validation node's primary claim is about how an institution or collective body operates — regardless of the ecological, social, or economic domain.

The Obuntu **Re-Generating** pathway addresses the same institutional continuity and succession from a relational perspective — asking not just whether governance structures exist, but whether they have the intergenerational relationships and community legitimacy to persist. The Obuntu **Re-Trusting** pathway is the relational counterpart to SDG16_1 — addressing trust restoration as a precondition for functional dispute resolution and participatory governance.

SDG 16 often co-applies with SDG 6 and SDG 15 — community water management and land stewardship governance bodies are precisely the institutions this schema evaluates.
